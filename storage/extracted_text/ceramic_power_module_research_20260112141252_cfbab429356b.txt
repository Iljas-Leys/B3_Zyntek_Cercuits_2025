Title: Thermal and Mechanical Performance of Ceramic Substrates for High-Temperature Power Electronics Abstract This paper investigates the suitability of ceramic-based substrates for power electronic modules operating at temperatures above 200°C. Alumina (Al2O3), aluminum nitride (AlN), and silicon nitride (Si3N4) substrates are evaluated in terms of thermal conductivity, mechanical strength, and long-term reliability. 1. Introduction The increasing demand for high-power-density electronics in automotive and aerospace applications requires substrates capable of operating under extreme thermal and mechanical stress. Ceramic substrates offer superior thermal stability compared to conventional FR-4 materials. 2. Materials and Methods Three ceramic materials were evaluated: - Alumina (Al2O3) - Aluminum Nitride (AlN) - Silicon Nitride (Si3N4) Samples were metallized using direct bonded copper (DBC) and tested under cyclic thermal loading between -40°C and 250°C. 3. Results Aluminum Nitride demonstrated the highest thermal conductivity (170–180 W/mK) but showed increased brittleness under mechanical shock. Silicon Nitride exhibited superior fracture toughness and long-term stability under thermal cycling. 4. Discussion The results indicate that ceramic substrate selection must balance thermal performance with mechanical robustness. For automotive traction inverters, Silicon Nitride substrates offer a favorable compromise. 5. Conclusion Ceramic-based PCBs represent a viable solution for high-temperature power electronics. Further work will investigate multilayer ceramic interconnects and embedded cooling channels. Keywords: Ceramic substrates, power electronics, thermal management, high-temperature PCB
